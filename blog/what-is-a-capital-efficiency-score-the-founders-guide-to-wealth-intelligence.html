Beyond Net Worth: Defining the Capital Efficiency Score

Net worth is a vanity metric. It tells you where you have been, but it says nothing about where you are going. For most founders, wealth is a collection of fragments: bank accounts, real estate holdings, crypto wallets, and business entities. This fragmentation creates a massive cognitive load. You see the "number go up," yet you feel a persistent unease that your capital is sitting idle. You're left asking, what is a capital efficiency score, and why does it matter more than the bottom line of a spreadsheet?

A Capital Efficiency Score (CES) is a dynamic measure of how hard your total wealth is actually working. It moves beyond the static balance sheet to evaluate liquidity, risk, and capital velocity. While traditional corporate finance uses metrics like Return on Capital Employed (ROCE) to measure business profitability, the CES applies this high-performance logic to your entire life. It treats your integrated personal and business net worth as a single engine. If that engine isn't tuned, you're losing power to friction and "lazy" capital.

The Problem with Static Net Worth

The "founder spreadsheet" is where data goes to die. It is manual, brittle, and outdated the moment you close the tab. Relying on static net worth leads to several critical failures:

  • Deceptive Growth: Your net worth can increase while your actual purchasing power stalls due to inflation or poor asset allocation.
  • Fragmented Visibility: Assets hidden in LLCs or physical collectibles often go unmonitored, leading to unoptimized "dead" zones.
  • Capital Drag: Holding too much cash in low-yield accounts or failing to rotate out of stagnant investments creates a silent tax on your growth.

Static tracking ignores the velocity of your money. It doesn't tell you if you're prepared for a liquidity event or if you're over-exposed to a single sector. You need a live picture, not a historical record.
