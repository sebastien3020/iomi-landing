How does the Capital Efficiency Score (CES) differ from a credit score?

A credit score measures your ability to borrow; the Capital Efficiency Score (CES) measures your ability to compound. The CES scores five factors on your real holdings — liquidity, idle capital, drag, risk balance and concentration — on a 0–100 scale. It alerts you to portfolio drag that a traditional credit report would ignore. While a credit score helps you get a loan, the CES helps you optimize the capital you already have to maximize growth and efficiency.
