# IOMI vs Traditional Wealth Management: What Actually Changes

Comparing **IOMI vs traditional wealth management** is not a fair fight in either direction. They are different tools built for different jobs. A traditional wealth manager is a person who advises you, executes for you, and charges a percentage of your assets for the relationship. IOMI is software: a wealth intelligence platform that shows you everything you own, personal and business, in one live view — and charges a flat subscription.

The honest question is not "which one wins?" It is "what does each one actually do, and which of those things do you need right now?" This guide walks through the real differences: the fee model, the visibility gap, the intelligence layer, and the cases where a human advisor still earns their fee. By the end you should know exactly where the line sits for your situation.

## Key Takeaways

- Traditional wealth management charges a percentage of assets (typically around 1% per year); IOMI charges a flat subscription — $29/mo Personal, $79/mo Business — that never scales with your net worth.
- Advisors report quarterly and mostly cover the assets they manage. IOMI connects 12,000+ institutions through Plaid in read-only mode and refreshes on every sync, covering bank accounts, brokerage, crypto, real estate, private equity, and collectibles.
- Traditional advisors rarely see your business. IOMI tracks personal and business side by side in separate compartments, with a consolidated view — built for founders whose largest asset is their company.
- IOMI replaces the "how am I doing?" conversation with the Capital Efficiency Score, a 0–100 health check across liquidity, idle capital, drag, risk balance, and concentration.
- IOMI does not replace an advisor for estate planning, complex tax strategy, or behavioral coaching. Many founders run both: the platform for daily oversight, the human for the big, rare decisions.

## What Traditional Wealth Management Actually Does Well

Start with credit where it is due. A good wealth manager brings judgment. They have watched clients panic-sell at bottoms and over-concentrate at tops, and they will talk you out of both. They coordinate with your estate attorney and your CPA. They handle paperwork you do not want to touch. For complex situations — a liquidity event, a trust structure, a cross-border estate — an experienced human is worth real money.

The relationship model also enforces discipline. A quarterly meeting where someone walks you through your portfolio is better than no review at all. For people who will never look at their finances voluntarily, paying someone to make them look has genuine value.

The problems start when you look at who this model was designed for: salaried executives with liquid portfolios, predictable income, and no operating business. If that describes you, traditional wealth management works roughly as advertised. If you are a founder, it starts to break in specific, predictable ways.

## Where the Traditional Model Breaks for Founders

### Your biggest asset is invisible to your advisor

Most founders hold the majority of their net worth in company equity. A traditional advisor manages your brokerage account and maybe your retirement accounts — the liquid slice. Your business, your real estate, your crypto, and your physical assets sit outside the reporting entirely. You get a beautifully formatted quarterly statement covering 30% of your actual wealth.

IOMI is built the other way around. It tracks bank and wallet accounts, brokerage, retirement, crypto exchange accounts and wallets, real estate with live valuations, private equity and company equity, physical assets and collectibles, and liabilities. Holdings are classified by what they are, not where they sit. The point is a complete picture, not a managed slice. If you want the deeper argument for this approach, read [the modern net worth tracker guide](/blog/the-modern-net-worth-tracker-a-founders-guide-to-wealth-intelligence).

### The reporting cadence is quarterly; your life is not

A founder's balance sheet moves weekly. A client payment lands, a funding round changes your equity value, a currency swings. Quarterly PDF reports were designed for a world where portfolios only held stocks and bonds and nothing urgent ever happened between meetings. IOMI syncs read-only data from 12,000+ institutions through Plaid, so the picture you look at reflects your accounts as of the latest sync — not as of last quarter's close.

### The incentives are misaligned at the edges

An advisor paid on assets under management has a structural reason to prefer that your money stays in managed accounts. Paying down your mortgage, investing in your own company, or buying a rental property all shrink the fee base. Most advisors are honest professionals. But the model taxes exactly the moves founders most often need to make.

## The Visibility Difference: A Managed Slice vs the Whole Balance Sheet

Put the two side by side on coverage alone:

- **A traditional advisor sees:** the accounts you moved to their custody, plus whatever you remember to mention in meetings.
- **IOMI sees:** every account you connect, read-only, across personal and business — with each business entity in its own compartment (up to 3 entities on the Business plan) and a consolidated view on top.

That separation matters more than it sounds. Founders constantly answer questions like "how much of my liquidity is actually mine, and how much belongs to the company?" A traditional statement cannot answer it because it never distinguishes the two. IOMI keeps personal and business tracked side by side and never mixed, which is the only honest way to compute a founder's real position. The security posture is deliberately narrow: connections are read-only via Plaid, IOMI never sees bank credentials, and it can never move money.

The second half of an advisor's value is interpretation — telling you what the numbers mean. IOMI ships that as product features rather than meetings:

- **IOMI AI** answers questions from your real holdings — never from estimates when data is missing. Personal plan users get 100 messages a day; Business users get 200 with priority.
- **The Daily Brief** surfaces what changed since yesterday, so review takes minutes instead of a quarterly afternoon.

None of this is advice. IOMI shows you your position and computes the consequences of moves you define. The judgment stays with you. For some people that is the whole point; for others it is the reason to keep a human in the loop.

## What Does Not Change: When You Still Want a Human

An honest comparison of **IOMI vs traditional wealth management** has to include the cases software does not cover:

- **Estate and trust planning.** Structuring inheritance, trusts, and charitable vehicles requires professionals. IOMI can show the assets involved; it cannot draft the documents.
- **Complex tax strategy.** IOMI is not a tax engine. Multi-state, multi-country, or exit-event tax planning belongs with a CPA or tax attorney.
- **Behavioral coaching.** If your risk is panic-selling in a drawdown, a person who answers the phone at 2 a.m. beats any dashboard.
- **Execution and delegation.** IOMI never executes trades or moves money. If you want someone else to do the doing, that is an advisor's job.

The practical pattern we see: founders use IOMI as the always-on system of record and keep (or hire) an advisor for the two or three genuinely hard decisions a year — now armed with a complete, current picture to bring to that conversation instead of a shoebox of statements.
