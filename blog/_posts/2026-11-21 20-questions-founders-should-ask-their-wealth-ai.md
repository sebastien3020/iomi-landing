# 20 Questions Founders Should Ask Their Wealth AI

Most people who get access to a wealth AI ask it one question — "how am I doing?" — get a pleasant summary, and stop. That is like hiring a brilliant operator and only asking them for the weather. The value of an AI that sits on top of your real holdings is not summary; it is interrogation. The right **AI financial questions** turn a passive dashboard into an active review of your entire financial life.

This guide gives you twenty questions worth asking, organized into five rounds: visibility, capital efficiency, risk, simulation, and trajectory. They are written for founders — people whose wealth spans personal accounts, business entities, private equity, and real assets — and they assume one non-negotiable property of the AI answering them: it answers from your actual data and says so when data is missing, rather than guessing. An AI that fills gaps with plausible estimates is not a wealth tool; it is a storyteller.

## Key Takeaways

* The best use of a wealth AI is interrogation, not summary — specific questions about your holdings, asked against live data.
* Round one is visibility: what do I own, where, in which entity — questions a founder should be able to answer in seconds but usually can't.
* Rounds two and three probe efficiency and risk: idle cash, drag, concentration, and what your liquidity actually covers.
* Simulation questions — "what happens if I move $100k?" — deserve deterministic answers, computed from stated assumptions, not probabilistic hand-waving.
* An AI worth trusting refuses to estimate when data is missing; treat that refusal as a feature and a prompt to connect what's absent.

## Why Questions Beat Dashboards

A dashboard shows you what its designer decided to show. A question-driven review shows you what you actually need to know today, and today's need changes: some weeks it is "where is my cash," other weeks it is "what would selling the rental do to my five-year picture."

There is a second, subtler benefit. Formulating a question forces you to notice what you are worried about. Founders who run a weekly ten-minute interrogation of their finances — a handful of questions, live data, done — consistently know things about their balance sheet that founders with prettier dashboards do not.

One caveat before the list. A general-purpose chatbot can discuss finance fluently, but it cannot see your accounts, so its answers are generic by construction. The questions below only work against an AI grounded in your holdings — the difference is unpacked in [AI financial assistant vs. ChatGPT](/blog/ai-financial-assistant-vs-chatgpt-for-money-questions). In IOMI's case, IOMI AI answers from your connected accounts and manual assets, and never estimates when data is missing.

## Round One: Visibility — Do I Actually Know What I Own?

**1. What is my total net worth right now, and how has it changed this month?**
The baseline question. The useful part is the second half — the delta and its cause. A number without attribution ("up $40k because the brokerage rallied and the SaaS distributed") is trivia.

**2. What do I own, personally, versus what my entities own?**
The founder question. Personal and business are legally separate; your mental model usually is not. An AI sitting on compartmentalized data — each entity its own unit, consolidated view on top — can answer instantly. If you cannot get a clean answer, the problem is structural, and [tracking personal vs. business assets](/blog/tracking-personal-vs-business-assets-the-founders-guide-to-total-oversight) is the fix.

**3. How much cash do I have across every account, everywhere?**
Not "in my checking account" — everywhere: personal accounts, each entity's operating accounts, brokerage sweep, exchange balances. Founders are routinely wrong about this number by tens of thousands in either direction.

**4. What is my liquid net worth — what could I actually access within a month?**
Total net worth flatters founders because the biggest line is illiquid company equity. The liquid figure is the one that determines your real options. The distinction gets a full treatment in [liquid vs. illiquid net worth](/blog/liquid-vs-illiquid-net-worth-why-the-split-matters).

**5. Which accounts haven't synced recently, and what am I not seeing?**
The honesty question. A consolidated picture is only as good as its freshest sync, and a trustworthy AI will tell you what is stale or missing rather than papering over it.

## Round Two: Capital Efficiency — Is My Money Working?

**6. Where is my capital sitting idle?**
The highest-yield question on this list. Idle cash — sitting in low- or no-yield accounts beyond any deliberate reserve — is the most common and most fixable leak in founder finances. The full anatomy of the problem is in [idle cash: how much is too much](/blog/idle-cash-how-much-is-too-much).

**7. What is my Capital Efficiency Score, and which factor is dragging it down?**
IOMI scores capital deployment 0–100 across five factors: liquidity, idle capital, drag, risk balance, and concentration. The score is the summary; the factor breakdown is the to-do list. One weak factor usually explains most of a low score.

**8. What is creating drag in my portfolio?**
Drag is the quiet category: capital positioned so it structurally underperforms its role — misplaced holdings, forgotten positions, cash pretending to be strategy. It rarely announces itself; you have to ask.

**9. If I had an extra $50k today, where should it go according to my own goals?**
The next-dollar question. Answered against your actual allocation and stated goals — the way IOMI's Next Dollar Allocation frames it — it converts abstract strategy into a concrete destination for real money. The reasoning pattern is explored in [how AI answers the next-dollar question](/blog/how-ai-answers-what-should-i-do-with-my-next-dollar).

**10. What am I paying in visible costs across my accounts?**
Account fees, fund expenses where visible, subscription products you forgot. Not everything is detectable from transaction data, but what is detectable is worth surfacing annually.

## Round Three: Risk — What Could Hurt Me?

**11. How concentrated am I, really?**
Count everything — company equity, RSUs, crypto, the rental — and most founders find one asset is 60–90% of net worth. That may be the strategy, but it should be a known number, not a vibe. See [concentration risk for founders](/blog/concentration-risk-for-founders-when-your-company-is-80-of-your-net-worth) for what to do with the answer.

**12. How many months of personal runway do I have if all business income stopped?**
The founder stress question: liquid personal assets divided by personal burn, assuming zero distributions. This number determines how much risk you can afford to take everywhere else.

**13. Which of my entities could not survive three slow months?**
Per-entity runway, asked entity by entity. A business quietly subsidized by its siblings should be a known fact, not a year-end surprise.

**14. What does my risk balance look like across asset classes?**
Not just concentration in one asset — the shape of the whole: how much sits in volatile assets versus stable ones, growth versus income, liquid versus locked.

**15. What personal guarantees or liabilities connect my entities back to me?**
Debt with personal guarantees pierces the compartment walls. An AI tracking your liabilities can list them; the follow-up planning belongs with your attorney and CPA.

## Round Four: Simulation — What Happens If?

These questions deserve deterministic answers: stated assumptions in, definite numbers out, same answer every time you ask. Probabilistic mush ("there's a 70% chance this helps") is not decision support. The case for determinism is made in [deterministic financial modeling](/blog/wealth-simulation-software-the-founders-guide-to-deterministic-financial-modeling).

**16. What happens to my five-year picture if I move $100k from business cash to my brokerage account?**
The classic reallocation. A simulator computes the trajectory change; you judge whether it is worth it.

**17. What if I sell the rental property next year?**
A planned event with a date and an amount, dropped into the projection. Ask it now, not the week you list the property. (The tax consequences of any sale are CPA territory — model the cash mechanics, then verify the tax side with your accountant.)

**18. What if my income drops 40% for a year?**
The stress test. Watching your projected cash line absorb a bad year — or fail to — tells you more about your required reserves than any rule of thumb. A structured version of this exercise is in [stress-testing your wealth plan](/blog/stress-testing-your-wealth-plan-before-life-does).

**19. Am I on track for my number, and what single change moves the date most?**
Goals with milestones, checked against the status-quo trajectory — then the sensitivity question: of the moves available to me, which one actually matters? Often the answer is not the one you were emotionally debating.

## Round Five: Trajectory — The Standing Question

**20. What changed since yesterday, and does any of it need me?**
The daily question, and deliberately the shortest. Most days the honest answer is "nothing needs you," and a good daily brief says exactly that in thirty seconds. The days it says otherwise are the days the whole system pays for itself. ## What a Good Answer Looks Like

Twenty questions are only as good as the answers. Hold your wealth AI to four standards.

**Grounded.** Every figure traces to a holding you can inspect. IOMI AI answers from your real accounts — connected read-only through Plaid across 12,000+ institutions, which can see balances and transactions but never move money and never see credentials.

**Honest about gaps.** When data is missing, the answer says so instead of estimating. A confident answer built on a guess is worse than no answer, because you cannot tell it apart from a grounded one.

**Deterministic where it computes.** Simulations and projections come from stated assumptions and arithmetic, reproducibly. You can disagree with an assumption and change it; you cannot argue with a vibe.

**Bounded.** A wealth AI describes, computes, and surfaces. It does not tell you what to do with your life — decisions stay yours, and personalized investment advice stays with licensed humans. Work in five rounds: visibility (what do I own, where, in which entity), efficiency (where is cash idle, what drags), risk (concentration, runway, guarantees), simulation (what happens if I move, sell, or lose income), and trajectory (am I on track, what changed today). Specific questions against live data beat "how am I doing" every time — the generic question gets a generic answer.

No — and be suspicious of one that does. A wealth AI's proper job is descriptive and computational: report your positions, score deployment, compute scenarios deterministically.
