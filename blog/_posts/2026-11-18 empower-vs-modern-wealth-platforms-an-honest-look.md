# Empower vs Modern Wealth Platforms: An Honest Look

Most people searching for an **empower alternative** are not angry at Empower. They started with the free dashboard — often years ago, back when it was Personal Capital — got real value from it, and then hit a ceiling. Maybe it was the advisory calls. Maybe it was watching a business account, a SAFE, or a watch collection sit awkwardly in a tool built around retirement accounts. Maybe it was simply outgrowing a free product's incentives.

This is an honest look at that decision. Empower does several things genuinely well, and for a large group of users it remains a sensible default. But its design center — a free aggregation dashboard funneling into a human advisory business — fits some financial lives much better than others. If your finances look like a founder's or a self-directed investor's, with entities, private equity, crypto, and no interest in delegating management, the calculus changes. Here is where the line actually falls.

## Key Takeaways

* Empower's free dashboard is genuinely good at what it was built for: aggregating retail accounts, tracking net worth, and running long-horizon retirement analysis. For a salaried household with mainstream accounts, it is hard to argue with free.
* The free product is the top of a funnel into Empower's wealth management business, which charges a percentage of managed assets. The dashboard's incentives, outreach, and feature priorities follow from that model.
* The retirement-first frame fits employees better than founders: private company equity, multiple entities, and lumpy income are edge cases in a tool centered on portfolio accounts and a retirement date.
* Modern flat-subscription platforms flip the model: you are the customer, not the lead. IOMI charges a flat monthly fee, never a percentage of assets, and manages nothing — it exists to give you oversight, not to win your portfolio.
* The honest decision rule: if you want free tracking of mainstream accounts or genuinely want a managed-portfolio relationship, Empower is a fine choice. If you are self-directed with a complex balance sheet, a purpose-built platform will fit better.

## What Empower Genuinely Does Well

Credit first, because it is substantial.

Empower's aggregation is mature. The dashboard links banks, brokerages, retirement accounts, credit cards, and loans, and presents a clean consolidated net worth view. This was the product that taught a generation of users that seeing everything in one place was even possible, and the core experience remains solid.

The retirement planner is the standout feature. It runs long-horizon analysis on your accounts against a retirement spending target, stress-tests the plan, and presents the result in a way normal humans can act on. For the core question it was designed to answer — am I on track to retire? — it is one of the better free tools available anywhere.

The fee analyzer deserves mention too: surfacing the expense ratios buried in funds and estimating their long-term cost has genuinely saved users real money. And the price of all of this is zero, which is not nothing. A salaried professional with a 401(k), an IRA, a brokerage account, and a mortgage gets a durable, useful, free financial dashboard. If that describes you, you may not need an alternative at all.

## The Business Model Is the Product

Here is the part any honest comparison has to say plainly: Empower's free dashboard exists to generate leads for Empower's advisory business, which manages client portfolios for a fee calculated as a percentage of assets. That is not a scandal — it is a disclosed, legitimate model, and the advisory service has real clients who chose it deliberately. But the model shapes the product in ways a user should understand.

First, the outreach. Cross a certain asset threshold and you become a prospect; users routinely describe the calls and emails offering a portfolio review. Some appreciate it. Self-directed investors mostly do not, and "how do I stop the advisor calls" is one of the more common questions long-time dashboard users ask.

Second, the feature priorities. A funnel product invests where prospects convert: portfolio analysis, retirement projections, fee comparisons against managed alternatives. It has little incentive to invest deeply in things that make you more self-sufficient — entity structures, alternative asset handling, deterministic scenario tools — because self-sufficiency is not the business.

Third, the frame. Everything orients toward the managed-money worldview: your wealth is a portfolio, the goal is retirement, and the implicit question is whether you should hand the wheel to a professional. That frame genuinely serves people who want it. The [software-versus-advisor decision](/blog/wealth-management-software-vs-advisor-the-2026-founders-framework) is a real one with legitimate answers on both sides. The problem is only when the frame is mismatched to the user — which brings us to founders.

## Where the Retirement Frame Breaks for Founders

Take a typical IOMI user: a founder with a company that is most of their net worth, two LLCs, a brokerage account, crypto on an exchange and in wallets, a rental property, and a few real collectibles. Run that balance sheet through a retirement-first aggregator and watch what falls through.

**The largest asset has no login.** Private company equity cannot be linked, so the biggest line on the balance sheet becomes a manual placeholder outside every calculation the tool actually runs. A retirement projection that ignores 70% of your net worth is not conservative — it is answering a different person's question.

**Entities have no home.** Personal and business accounts land in one undifferentiated list. There is no concept of an entity as a compartment — no clean answer to "what does the business hold versus what do I hold," which is the first question a founder's balance sheet raises. Purpose-built platforms treat [personal-versus-business separation](/blog/tracking-personal-vs-business-assets-the-founders-guide-to-total-oversight) as architecture, not as an account label.

**The questions are different.** A founder's live questions are not "am I on track for 65?" They are: how concentrated am I across everything? How much cash is idle across entities? What happens to my five-year picture if I take a $200k distribution and redeploy it? Those need concentration analysis across all asset classes, idle-capital detection, and a deterministic simulator — none of which is what a retirement planner is for.

**Manual assets are dead weight.** Real estate, collectibles, and crypto in self-custody can often be added as static entries, but they sit inert — outside the planner, outside the analysis, updated never. A tool tells you what it values by what it computes on, and a retirement-first dashboard computes on portfolio accounts.

None of this makes Empower bad. It makes it specialized — for a financial life shaped like employment. When your life is shaped like ownership, you feel the mismatch weekly.

## The Decision, Honestly

Strip away the marketing on all sides and the choice resolves on two questions.

**Question one: who manages the money?** If the answer is "a professional, and I want that relationship," Empower's integrated dashboard-plus-advisor model is coherent and convenient — evaluate its advisory fees against independent advisors and decide. If the answer is "me," then a funnel product will always be slightly misaligned with you, and the recurring nudge toward management is a cost you pay in attention.

**Question two: what shape is your balance sheet?** Mainstream accounts and a retirement horizon: the free dashboard serves you well, and inertia is a legitimate strategy. Entities, private equity, crypto, real assets, lumpy income: you are the edge case in a retail tool, and a platform that treats your structure as the core case — the kind explored in the [modern net worth tracker guide](/blog/the-modern-net-worth-tracker-a-founders-guide-to-wealth-intelligence) — will fit the way a tailored suit fits versus a borrowed one.

Plenty of people run both for a transition period: Empower for its familiar retirement lens, IOMI as the system of record for the full balance sheet. The migration usually completes itself — whichever tool answers your real questions becomes the one you open.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Empower really free?

The dashboard is genuinely free to use. The business model behind it is advisory: Empower offers wealth management for a fee based on a percentage of managed assets, and dashboard users above certain asset levels are contacted about that service. You are not charged for tracking — but the product exists to introduce you to management, and its design reflects that.

### What is the best Empower alternative for founders?

For self-directed people whose wealth spans personal and business — entities, private equity, crypto, real estate, collectibles — a flat-subscription wealth platform like IOMI fits the structure better: entity compartments, all asset classes as first-class citizens, deterministic simulation, and no advisory funnel. For pure budgeting needs, Monarch is the stronger pick. The right alternative depends on which ceiling you hit.

### Does IOMI manage my investments like Empower's advisory service?

No. IOMI manages nothing and never touches your money — connections are read-only through Plaid, and IOMI cannot execute trades or move funds. It is an oversight and intelligence layer for people who direct their own wealth. If you want discretionary management, you need an advisory relationship; IOMI's role is making sure whoever decides — you or a professional — sees the complete picture.

### Can Empower track my business accounts and private equity?

You can often link business bank accounts and add manual placeholders for private assets, but the tool has no entity concept — everything lands in one personal-shaped list, and manual assets sit outside the analytical features. Platforms built for founders compartmentalize each entity separately with a consolidated view, and treat private equity and collectibles as tracked asset classes rather than footnotes.

### Why pay for IOMI when Empower is free?

If free covers your needs, don't pay — that is the honest answer. You are also buying an incentive structure: a flat fee means the product serves you, not a sales funnel.

### Is Empower's retirement planner better than IOMI's Projection?

They answer different questions. Empower's planner asks whether your portfolio supports a retirement spending target — a question it handles well for mainstream portfolios. IOMI's Projection shows the year-by-year path of your entire net worth from today's actual holdings, and the Simulator layers deterministic what-ifs on top: reallocations, planned events, distributions. Founders tend to need the second frame; traditional retirees the first.
