What the Capital Efficiency Score measures

Net worth is a single number that answers one question: how much do you have. It says nothing about whether that capital is doing anything. Two people with identical net worth can be in completely different positions — one with everything in a savings account earning nothing, the other with a mix that matches their horizon and their appetite for risk.

The Capital Efficiency Score is a 0–100 measure of the gap between the portfolio you have and the portfolio your own profile implies. It is not a performance score and not a prediction. It is a structural diagnosis, and it is deterministic: the same inputs always give the same score.

The score is built by measuring five inefficiencies, weighting them, and collapsing them into one number. A perfectly aligned portfolio has zero inefficiency and scores 100. Every gap between where you are and where your profile points costs you points.

What a good score looks like

The app groups scores into three bands, and this page uses the same thresholds.

ScoreBandWhat it means
75–100OptimizedCapital is efficiently deployed, with balanced structure and allocation. There is usually still one factor worth looking at.
50–74ModerateRoom for improvement in capital efficiency and allocation. Typically one or two factors carrying most of the drag.
0–49Needs AttentionCapital structure needs attention. Usually a large idle cash position, a heavy concentration, or no core exposure at all.

A high score is not the goal in itself. A portfolio can score well and still be wrong for you, because the score measures alignment with the profile you entered — not whether that profile is the right one.

Why founders score differently

The model has three places where an entrepreneur's situation shows up, and they mostly work in your favour once the inputs are honest.

  • Variable income raises your cash targetIncome stability feeds the risk target. Low or Medium reduces target risk, which raises the cash sleeve. A cash pile that looks lazy on a salaried profile can be correct on a founder's.
  • Business equity is concentrationIf a large share of your wealth is one company, the concentration factor will say so. That is not a recommendation to sell — it is the model naming a real exposure that most tools never see.
  • The invisible half changes everythingProperty, private holdings, physical assets. Leave them out and the score is computed on a fraction of your balance sheet, and the answer is meaningless.

What this calculator does not do

The app scores holding by holding. This page scores asset-class buckets. For expected return, volatility and expense drag those are mathematically the same thing whenever no per-holding override exists, because each holding falls back to its class default. Two places are genuinely different, and both are handled by asking you directly instead of guessing: concentration, and how liquid your cash actually is inside its account wrapper.

It also does not model covariance between asset classes. Portfolio risk is computed as if each asset class moved independently of the others. They do not. This is a deliberate MVP simplification, it is the same one the production engine makes, and it means the risk figure is a structural indicator rather than a forecast of how the portfolio will actually move.

It produces no recommendations. It will not tell you what to buy, what to sell, or what to do next. That is the paid product, and it is a different kind of claim.

Questions people ask

What is a good capital efficiency score? Anything from 75 up is "Optimized" in IOMI's bands. Most portfolios that have never been deliberately structured land somewhere in the 50s and 60s, usually because of idle cash or a single dominant position.

How is capital efficiency calculated? Five inefficiencies — idle capital, risk balance, liquidity, drag, concentration — each measured as a gap between your portfolio and the target your profile implies. Idle capital weighs the most, then risk balance, liquidity, drag and concentration. The bigger the gaps, the lower the score.

Why does my business cash hurt my score? It usually doesn't — cash held against a real emergency-fund target and known upcoming expenses is exactly what the model wants. Cash beyond that target isn't penalised by the liquidity factor either. What it does affect is idle capital and risk balance: a large cash position pulls your portfolio's risk below the target your profile implies, and that gap is what costs points.

Does anything I type get sent anywhere? No. The whole calculation runs in your browser. The only thing that leaves the page is the link you copy, and only if you choose to copy it.
