The part a simulator can't do for you

Every number above starts with one you had to guess: what you actually own today. For a founder that is the hardest figure in the whole model — the operating account, the holding company, the property, the vested equity, the things with no ticker. IOMI keeps that number current from your connected accounts and the assets you enter by hand, personal and business in one place, so the starting point stops being a guess.

What this simulator does, and what it deliberately doesn't

It takes what you own today, splits it into market assets and everything else, compounds each at the rate you set, adds your yearly contributions, and applies a flat tax drag to the growth. It then shows the result twice: in future dollars, and in today's dollars once inflation has been taken out. The second number is the one that means something, and it is the one most projection tools bury.

It does not model sequence-of-returns risk, market crashes, changing contributions, a liquidity event, salary growth, or the tax treatment of any specific account. It does not know whether your business equity is worth what you think it is. Those are the things that decide the actual outcome, and no slider can stand in for them.

Why founders need a different projection

The standard retirement calculator assumes a salary, a 401(k), and a portfolio that is entirely liquid and entirely visible. That describes almost none of the people this tool is built for.

  • Your wealth is not in one placePersonal accounts, an operating company, a holding structure, property, sometimes a second entity. The total only exists once someone adds it up.
  • Much of it has no price feedBusiness equity, private holdings, real estate, physical assets. A brokerage app shows you the visible half and calls it your net worth.
  • Your contributions are lumpyA good quarter, a distribution, a slow year. A flat annual figure is a simplification, and it is worth knowing that it is one.

The three numbers to actually look at

What it earns. Over a long horizon this usually overtakes both what you started with and what you added. If it doesn't in your scenario, the horizon is short or the return assumption is doing the work.

Today's dollars. A seven-figure number thirty years out is not a seven-figure number. Inflation at 2.5% halves purchasing power in about 28 years. The real figure is the honest one.

Monthly income. Net worth is a stock; what you can spend is a flow. The withdrawal rate turns one into the other, and it is the number that tells you whether the plan works.

A note on the defaults. 7% for market assets is roughly the long-run real-ish figure people quote for broad equity, before tax. 5% for everything else is deliberately unexciting. 18.8% tax is the US long-term capital gains rate plus the net investment income tax. None of these are predictions — change them and watch how much the answer moves. That sensitivity is the most useful thing this page can show you.

Questions people ask

Is a 4% withdrawal rate safe? It is a widely cited rule of thumb from research on historical US portfolios over 30-year windows. It is not a guarantee, it assumes a particular asset mix, and it says nothing about your tax situation or your spending. Treat it as a reference point, not a plan.

Should business equity really be in here? If you own it, it is part of your balance sheet. The honest caveats are that its value is an estimate until someone buys it, and that it is illiquid — which is exactly why it sits on the "everything else" side of the split rather than the market side.

Why is the tax applied to growth every year? Because it is the simplest assumption that is not wrong in your favour. In reality tax is deferred until you sell, which helps you, and rates differ by account and holding period. Modelling it as an annual drag makes the projection slightly conservative rather than slightly optimistic.

Does anything I type get sent anywhere? No. The whole calculation runs in your browser. The only thing that leaves the page is the link you copy, and only if you choose to copy it.
