IOMI vs Wealthfront

Different categories, not direct competitors: Wealthfront manages a slice of your money; IOMI is the intelligence layer over everything you own. Here is where each one belongs.

5 min read · Updated August 23, 2026

The short version — different categories

Wealthfront is an automated investment manager (a robo-advisor): it custodies your money, invests it in diversified index portfolios for a 0.25% annual advisory fee on investment accounts, and offers a high-yield cash account. It does that job very well. IOMI is not in that category: it manages nothing and custodies nothing. It is the read-only intelligence layer over everything you own — including a Wealthfront account — for a flat subscription. This is not an either/or page.

Most comparisons on this site put IOMI next to other trackers. Wealthfront is a different kind of product. When you open a Wealthfront investment account, you transfer money to it; Wealthfront holds it and manages it inside diversified index portfolios. That is asset management — a real service, priced as a percentage of the assets it manages.

IOMI does the opposite job. It never touches your money: connections are read-only via Plaid, and IOMI cannot custody assets or execute trades — structurally, not just as a policy. What it does is see everything at once — bank, brokerage (including a Wealthfront account), real estate, crypto, company equity, collectibles, and business entities (up to 3, never mixed with personal) — and turn that whole picture into decisions: the Capital Efficiency Score (0–100, computed on five factors: liquidity, idle capital, drag, risk balance, concentration), Next Dollar Allocation, a deterministic Simulator, Goals, and IOMI AI, which answers from your real holdings.

Can you use both?

Yes — many people should. Wealthfront manages a slice of your money; IOMI shows you the whole picture, including that slice. If Wealthfront runs your index portfolio well, nothing about IOMI asks you to change that. IOMI simply puts that portfolio next to the assets no manager sees — the property, the company equity, the idle cash across entities — and scores how hard the whole balance sheet is working.

Who should pick which

  • Pick Wealthfront if you want your investing done for you: hands-off, diversified index portfolios and a high-yield cash account, for a 0.25% advisory fee on what it manages. It is very good at that job.
  • Pick IOMI if your wealth is bigger than any one account — property, entities, equity, collectibles — and you want one score, one simulator and one AI over all of it, without handing anything over.
  • Pick both if Wealthfront runs your portfolio and you still lack the whole picture. IOMI sits over every account you own — the Wealthfront one included — and shows how the pieces work together.
